## Treatment paths and confounding

Cell 1 contains SIM_CONFIG, all equations, FarmerAgent, FarmModel and run_sim. `strategy_type` controls social scoring in decide_crop, premiums/costs/losses in harvest, send eligibility in FarmModel.step, recipient eligibility in propagate_knowledge, and membership of zone aggregates in update_shared_knowledge. Initialization creates alternating SHARED/INDIVIDUAL pairs. Remaining strategy checks partition reporting and plots (cells 2, 3, 5–7, 9, 11–12); dashboard_solara.py presents exported results rather than executing the experiment.

The executable defaults are price +10%, shared cost factor 0.80, individual factor 0.90, loss 6% versus 12%. Shared cost is thus 11.11% below the individual cost, not 30%. README says +20%, 30% reduction, 5% versus 15%. Protocol repeats README numbers as context. Code values define the primary experiment, as instructed; scientific review was requested and the user requested a separate README sensitivity. That sensitivity interprets 30% as relative to individual realized cost: 0.63 versus 0.90. This interpretation is explicit, not evidence of a historical parameter set.

Zone observations are last-season mean gross yield and **total farm profit**, not profit per hectare; land therefore influences social profit signals. Neighbors expose their entire knowledge bases. Propagation adds harvest tuples directly into recipients' own knowledge bases; these then enter the own-experience bonus as well as later neighbor reads. Repeated and indirectly shared observations are not independent or deduplicated. min_neighbor_obs gates neighbor scoring, not propagation despite its comment. `weighted` and `none` filtering behave identically; only `filter` enforces same-zone contacts. Graphs include both original strategies; SHARED can read INDIVIDUAL own knowledge through neighbor reads.

Cell 9's pre-existing three-scenario ablation zeros social score weights for economics-only but continues propagation into the own-experience bonus. It therefore does not remove social information. It also lacks the four independent worlds and factorial interaction. Scenario capitalization in the plotting loop differs from two stored labels, producing empty subsets. Cell 5's agent-level Welch test ignores within-run dependence. Cells 6, 11, 12 repeat seeds but report percent advantage without paired factorial uncertainty; the function called literature calibration merely assigns scenario constants and does not fit or validate them.

## Randomness, initialization, and timing

V1 assigns pairs a shared zone, land, budget, goal and irrigation type, but independent global Gaussian cost noise and distinct suitability noise keyed by unique ID. `hash((id, crop, "suit"))` varies across Python processes and is not controlled by random_seed. Cost noise hard-codes 0.15 even though cost_noise_std exists. A model-local random stream handles population draws, zone weather and shuffled order; global random handles farmer cost and harvest variability. Mesa is constructed without its seed. Global reseeding makes interleaved runs unsafe. Odd n_agents silently drops one agent. Optional missing NetworkX silently disables the network; requirements omits an explicit NetworkX dependency and allows Mesa versions too old for AgentSet.

Each season sets zone weather, adjusts irrigation (0.6 drought; 0.9 otherwise), decides all crops before any harvest, harvests all, refreshes zone aggregates, then propagates knowledge. There is no within-season future-outcome access. Global draws depend on harvest ordering. Own and received observations accumulate; zone observations cover only the last season. No wealth accumulation, budget constraint, entry/exit, adaptive trust, exploration or reinforcement learning is implemented. Max-score ties follow crop-list insertion order.

## Equations and provenance

Gross yield = fixed crop baseline × viable-zone multiplier (1 or .2) × soil multiplier (1 or .4) × climate multiplier (1, .7 or .4) × weather uniform multiplier × truncated Gaussian noise. Weather multiplier ranges: drought .3–.6, wet 1–1.2, normal .8–1.1. Climate uses static annual summaries and simulated categorical seasons, not actual yearly sequences. Suitability uses different climate/soil scoring, moisture mean, and a fixed farmer/crop noise. Moisture standard deviation, variability_min/max and many dataset fields are unused.

Raw cost/ha = labor days × SMAG + category fertilizer + category seed + category mechanization + water_need_mm × tariff × 10; then farmer cost noise with lower multiplier bound .5; then treatment cost efficiency. Profit = (gross yield × (1-loss) × treatment price − treatment cost) × land. Intended water proxy = categorical crop water need × irrigation level; however water_need_mm is nested under cost_model in the JSON while both V1 helpers read the top level. All crops therefore fall back to 500 mm for cost and water proxy. This lookup discrepancy is preserved; irrigation affects neither yield nor billed water cost. This inconsistency is preserved and precludes interpreting the proxy as validated withdrawal or sustainability. No emissions, biodiversity, soil evolution, water scarcity or sustainability outcome is calculated.

The four JSON inputs contain six zones and 24 crops. Their metadata attributes climate to NASA POWER 2000–2023, moisture to processed Copernicus CSV rzsm_1, ecological ranges to EcoCrop, and yields/prices to FAOSTAT. These are repository provenance claims, not externally revalidated observations. Prices include different years and proxy mappings (e.g. clementines use oranges); baseline yields are approximate national averages. scripts/fetch_climate_data.py fetches and summarizes monthly point estimates; scripts/extract_morocco_data.ipynb processes moisture; extract_faostat_prices.py reads bulk Africa price tables; update_crops_faostat.py hard-codes selected prices and cost assumptions into JSON. These scripts are not executed or data altered. Raw NetCDF and price archives are not needed at runtime.

Empirical-attributed inputs: climate summaries, moisture summaries, EcoCrop ranges, selected prices, wage. Derived inputs/outcomes: climate frequencies and summaries, moisture means, additive production costs and simulated profit. Hypothetical/unvalidated assumptions: cost category constants and proxies, yield multipliers, Gaussian variation, fixed scoring weights, goal mixture, graph, land/zone distributions, treatment economics, annual switching including perennial crops. README's “real cost” and convergence claims exceed demonstrated validation; its paired agents are only partially matched. It also references an extraction notebook at the wrong location. No external source verification is claimed here.

## Deliberate experiment changes

1. Independent information/economics switches replace behavioral strategy labels; controls neither publish/receive social observations nor read zone/neighbors. Own-experience scoring stays on in every world. V1's mixed observation memory and repeated-message behavior stay in information-on worlds.
2. Each world has n_agents identities, all eligible for its assigned treatment. Identity is matched across A–D, not two mixed groups within one world. This is a population-level policy estimand with interference inside worlds; it does not reproduce the mixed-population V1 contrast. Cooperative eligibility is externally assigned and not earned by sharing. Graph density/topology rules are retained over that population.
3. SHA-256 keyed model-local streams replace salted hashes/global RNG. Streams separate population, farmer cost, suitability, weather, order and harvest by seed/identity/season. Harvest common shocks are crop-independent, preserving V1 marginal distributions while changing coupling. Cost noise now honors cost_noise_std (same default .15). Mesa is seeded. No bitwise equivalence to old notebook outputs is claimed.
4. Separate fresh zones, memories, graphs, configs and RNGs per run. Missing network dependency fails explicitly; invalid size/options fail rather than silently degrading the design.
5. Raw agent-season outcomes precede aggregation; inference uses independent seed runs rather than correlated agents. Notebook and original exports are untouched.

The moisture extraction notebook present in scripts stops at filtering/plotting a NetCDF-derived DataFrame; it does not contain a complete regeneration recipe for the committed zone-profile JSON. Reproducibility here is conditional on the versioned JSON, whose hashes are recorded.

Saharan climate is a sparser profile than the other zones: it has no drought_frequency or wet_frequency, so generate_weather uses the V1 defaults .20 and .15 there. This fallback is preserved and is a hypothetical component of that zone's environmental trajectory.
